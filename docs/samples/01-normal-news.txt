Health officials in the northern region said on Tuesday that a measles outbreak has spread to three more towns, with 42 confirmed cases since the start of the month. The regional health department said most of the infected children had not received the recommended vaccine doses, and it has opened extra clinics to offer free vaccinations to families over the next two weeks.

"Measles spreads very easily through the air, and the best protection we have is two doses of the vaccine," said Dr. Helen Marsh, the department's director of public health. She said staff were contacting schools and nurseries to identify children who may have been exposed and to advise families on what to do if symptoms appear, including fever, cough and a rash.

The department said that no deaths have been reported so far, although seven patients have required treatment in hospital. Local schools will keep open as planned, but unvaccinated children who have been in contact with a confirmed case will be asked to stay at home for up to three weeks. A spokesperson added that the outbreak is being monitored daily and that updated figures will be published on the department's website each Friday.
