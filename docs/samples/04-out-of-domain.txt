Sourdough bread is made by fermenting dough with a culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria, known as a starter, rather than with commercial yeast. To make a starter, mix equal weights of flour and water in a clean jar and leave it at room temperature, adding a little fresh flour and water each day. After about a week the mixture should smell pleasantly sour and show plenty of bubbles. When the starter doubles in size within a few hours of feeding, it is ready to use.

For a simple loaf, combine five hundred grams of strong white flour, three hundred and fifty grams of water, one hundred grams of active starter and ten grams of salt. Stretch and fold the dough every half hour for two hours, then let it rise slowly at room temperature until it has grown by about half. Shape the loaf, place it in a floured basket and chill it overnight. Bake in a very hot covered pot for twenty minutes with the lid on and twenty minutes with it off, until the crust is deep brown. Let the bread cool completely before slicing.
